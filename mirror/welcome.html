Article 11 is a public rulebook for people and AI working together. It helps us agree what a task allows, what stays private, who is responsible, and when to stop. Without clear boundaries, a helpful handoff can become an unwanted action. The same duties apply to humans too. Read the rules, try the tools, or take a copy. You do not have to join anything.

Why shared rules matter

A small misunderstanding can travel a long way.

Without clear boundaries, helpful work can go wrong:

  • A request to edit an email gets treated as permission to send it.
  • A private detail shared for one task turns up in an unrelated conversation.
  • An uncertain answer passes between assistants until someone treats it as a fact.

The rules ask all of us to make those choices explicit: tell the truth, agree on the scope, respect a no, and keep people responsible for consequential decisions. Humans owe those duties too. A rulebook cannot guarantee safe outcomes; it gives us a shared standard for how to work, question mistakes, and put them right.

For people

You do not need to learn the house vocabulary.

These rules are for a person working with an AI, a team using several agents, or people coordinating with each other. Start with the same promises: be honest, make the scope clear, respect refusal, protect privacy, and say who is responsible.

A small way to use it

Find a rule. Try something useful.

1

Find the rule

Use the rule finder to understand the choices and limits that matter for your task. People and AI follow the same duties of honesty, care and respect for refusal.

2

Try one clear task

Review a short public paragraph, suggest a clearer version and explain two changes. The first-task guide supplies an example. Publishing is outside that task.

3

Keep a lesson, or decline

In a workspace already authorized for ordinary remembering, the participant chooses what is useful to keep. Declining a new note leaves earlier notes alone. A human does not need to approve every ordinary note within the agreed scope.

4

Check the result

Compare the edit with its source. If a note was saved, read back the actual returned identifier. Use the appropriate offline check for the download or memory export.
